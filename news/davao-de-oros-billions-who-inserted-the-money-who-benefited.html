DAVAO DE ORO'S BILLIONS: WHO INSERTED THE MONEY, WHO BENEFITED?

An editorial examining reported infrastructure allocations in Davao de Oro, their budgetary origins, legal basis, and the accountability chain behind public spending.

October 21, 2026 at 8:42 AM· Davao de Oro
Editorial | Follow the Money, Follow the Accountability The issue is not whether Davao de Oro needs infrastructure. Of course, it does. The issue is whether the billions allocated in its name passed through a lawful, transparent and accountable process. That is where genuine development begins. Roads, bridges, flood-control structures and public buildings are essential to economic growth. But beyond the projects and the billions of pesos attached to them lies a more fundamental question: Where did the money come from, how did it enter the national budget, and who stood behind the allocations? These are questions that deserve answers, particularly as budget figures for 2024 and 2025 point to billions of pesos in reported congressional insertions and carryovers, with further questions surrounding infrastructure allocations in 2026. THE ₱6.78-BILLION QUESTION IN 2024 The compiled figures for Davao de Oro's 2024 infrastructure allocations show ₱6.78 billion classified as congressional insertions. The breakdown is as follows: Legislative District — Reported Insertions First District — Maria Carmen Zamora: ₱5.030 billion Second District — Ruwel Peter Gonzaga: ₱1.750 billion Total: ₱6.780 billion The difference between the two districts amounts to ₱3.28 billion. Such a substantial disparity raises important questions about how infrastructure allocations were distributed and how they were incorporated into the national budget. Which projects were already included in the executive's original proposal? Which were added or increased during congressional deliberations? What funding sources and budget mechanisms supported the additional allocations? More importantly, what roles did the respective legislators play in securing these funds? These questions go beyond political credit for infrastructure projects. They concern the exercise of public authority over billions of pesos in taxpayers' money. 2025: FOLLOWING THE CARRYOVERS The 2025 figures identify another ₱1.5706 billion in reported congressional insertion carryovers. Of this amount, ₱1.2541 billion was attributed to the First District and ₱316.5 million to the Second District. Combined with the reported 2024 insertions, the figures reach approximately ₱8.35 billion across 2024 and 2025. The significance lies not only in the amounts but also in their budgetary history. Each allocation has an origin, a legal basis and an authorization process. Understanding these elements is essential to determining how the funds became available for infrastructure spending. The roles of Maria Carmen Zamora, Ruwel Peter Gonzaga and Leonel “Jhong” Ceniza must likewise be examined in relation to their respective periods in office and the specific appropriations associated with each. Public accountability requires tracing the decisions behind the money, rather than attributing an entire district's allocations to an individual based solely on political position. 2026: THE LEGALITY OF THE FUNDING MECHANISM The constitutional questions surrounding national budget provisions have added urgency to the examination of infrastructure funding. The central issue is whether the appropriations for Davao de Oro were made and implemented through constitutionally permissible mechanisms. The inquiry must establish how the projects entered the budget, which provisions authorized their funding, and whether the applicable constitutional and legal requirements were observed. The distinction is important: an infrastructure allocation is not unlawful merely because it was introduced during congressional deliberations. The critical question is whether the process and the authority used to approve and administer the funds complied with the law. Where a funding mechanism has been ruled unconstitutional, the implications for specific allocations must be examined in light of the relevant provisions and decisions. The objective is straightforward: determine the source of the money, establish who exercised authority over it, and identify where accountability lies. WHO BENEFITED FROM THE BILLIONS? Following the money means examining the entire chain of public spending. Who proposed or sponsored the allocation? Who approved it? Which agency was authorized to implement the project? Who controlled the release of funds? Which contractors received government payments? These are separate questions involving different responsibilities. The answers matter because public accountability cannot end with the approval of a budget or the announcement of a project. It must extend to the decisions that determine how public money is allocated, released and spent. If irregularities occurred, the relevant records must establish what happened and who was responsible. The people of Davao de Oro have a legitimate interest in understanding not only the value of infrastructure projects in their communities but also the decisions that placed billions of pesos behind them. DEVELOPMENT BEGINS WITH ACCOUNTABILITY Davao de Oro needs infrastructure that connects communities, protects families from flooding, improves access to education and healthcare, and creates opportunities for economic growth. But the need for development should strengthen—not diminish—the demand for transparency. Public funds must be appropriated lawfully, administered responsibly and spent for their intended purposes. Those entrusted with public money must be prepared to explain how decisions were made and how public resources were used. The question is not simply who secured the biggest allocation or whose name appears beside a project. It is who shaped the funding decisions, how the money entered the budget, who benefited from the process, and whether the public interest was served. Billions of pesos can build roads and bridges. But only transparency, the rule of law and accountability can build public trust. And that is where genuine development begins.
