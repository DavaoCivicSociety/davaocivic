Davao Oriental Beautiful: Beyond Branding, Toward Sustainable Tourism

The province-wide tourism campaign could support local businesses and investment, but its lasting success will depend on infrastructure, environmental protection, visitor management, and sharing tourism benefits with communities across Davao Oriental.

October 20, 2026 at 12:18 PM· Davao Oriental
DAVAO ORIENTAL — The provincial government’s “Davao Oriental Beautiful” campaign represents more than a tourism promotion initiative. It signals a broader strategy to strengthen the local economy, attract investments, and position the province as a competitive tourism destination. With its rich biodiversity, pristine beaches, and natural attractions, Davao Oriental has significant potential to generate economic opportunities through tourism. Establishing a unified tourism identity could help local businesses attract visitors and encourage spending in transportation, accommodation, food services, and community enterprises. However, effective branding is only the beginning. The success of the campaign will depend on the government's ability to address infrastructure gaps, improve waste management, strengthen emergency services, and protect environmentally sensitive destinations such as Mount Hamiguitan, Pujada Bay, Aliwagwag Falls, and Dahican Beach. Sustainable tourism also requires clear visitor management policies to prevent overcrowding and environmental degradation. At the same time, tourism revenues must benefit communities beyond Mati City, ensuring that smaller and remote municipalities share in the province's economic growth. One promising approach is community-based tourism, which allows residents to participate directly as tour guides, homestay operators, local food producers, artisans, and environmental stewards. By empowering local communities, the province can help ensure that tourism income circulates within local economies while creating employment opportunities for women, young people, and other sectors. Ultimately, “Davao Oriental Beautiful” offers an opportunity to transform the province's natural wealth into lasting economic benefits. But its long-term success will depend not only on how effectively the province promotes its attractions, but also on how well it protects them and distributes the gains. Beyond a beautiful image, Davao Oriental must build a tourism industry that is sustainable, inclusive, and beneficial to its people.
