When The Road Hides — Silence Is Not An Answer

An editorial raises transparency questions about a road project in Barangay Cabangcalan, recounts allegations attributed to the barangay captain, and calls on municipal officials to release project and procurement documents and respond publicly.

October 20, 2026 at 12:09 PM· Banaybanay, Davao Oriental
📝 EDITORIAL: When The Road Hides — Silence Is Not An Answer 📍 Banaybanay, Davao Oriental | 📅 October 20, 2026 Construction has begun in Barangay Cabangcalan. Concrete is being poured. Heavy equipment moves through the community. Yet the person the people elected to lead them — Kapitan Jun — says he was never told. No notice. No plans. No budget breakdown. No signboard explaining what is being built or at what cost. This is not a misunderstanding. This is a question of what public service actually means. 🛣️ THE PROJECT THAT NO ONE CAN EXPLAIN A road project should be the most visible thing in a barangay — not the most secretive. Residents should be able to stand there and read exactly what is happening, who is doing it, how much it costs, and who to call if something is wrong. That is not an unreasonable expectation. That is the law. Instead, the people’s elected representative says he was kept entirely in the dark. He knows only what the workers told him: the contractor is someone named “Bandigan.” That is all. No business address. No bidding records. No contract copy. No one even seems willing or able to provide basic information that should be posted on a signboard for anyone to read. 💬 BEHIND THE SILENCE: A DISPUTE OVER AID Reports suggest this exclusion did not begin with a road. It began when ₱3 million in food assistance arrived for Cabangcalan. Kapitan Jun reportedly insisted that the funds be used to provide one sack of rice per family — a reasonable allocation given the budget. Mayor Ian Larcia, it is claimed, directed that only five kilograms be distributed per household. Kapitan Jun refused to compromise. Since that refusal, Kapitan Jun says he has been systematically cut off: aid routed around him, flood assistance denied to his constituents, his office bypassed even for basic coordination. Now a road project proceeds without his knowledge. If true, this is not governance. It is leverage. Using public resources to pressure an elected official into submission is not how public service works. It is abuse of authority — and it deserves to be called exactly that. ⚖️ TRANSPARENCY IS NOT A FAVOR — IT IS THE RULE The laws are clear: - The Local Government Code mandates that barangay officials are the first point of coordination for projects in their own jurisdiction - Procurement laws require public bidding, documented contracts, and full disclosure - COA rules mandate visible signage — so every resident knows where their money is going When these rules are ignored, we are not seeing “efficiency” or “getting things done.” We are seeing accountability being bypassed by design. If the project is legitimate, the documents exist. If the process was proper, the records prove it. There is no honest reason to keep them hidden. 🗣️ THE ANSWER IS SIMPLE No one needs to be condemned today. Mayor Larcia’s office has every right — and every opportunity — to respond. Release the Program of Works. Show the Detailed Engineering Design. Identify the contractor publicly. Post the signboard. Explain why the Barangay Captain was not notified. Transparency requires no defense. Secrecy always does. If the documents are complete, show them. If the procurement was legal, prove it. If there was a misunderstanding, explain it. But do not tell the people of Cabangcalan — or anyone in Banaybanay — that they have no right to know what is happening in their own community with their own money. Because at the end of the day, the road does not belong to the contractor. It does not belong to the Mayor. It belongs to the people who paid for it — and they have a right to see it built in the open. “Government does not exist to be served. It exists to serve. And when it stops answering to the people — it stops being government at all.” The documents are waiting. The people are watching. The answer is overdue. #ProjectsBuiltInTheDark #BanaybanayNeedsAnswers #TransparencyIsNotOptional #CabangcalanDeservesBetter
